## 1. What the project does

**One sentence.** Take 4.85M public NSW property transfer records and build a house-price
analysis that uses **no future information**, then test the three hypotheses the group's EDA
proposed.

**Four layers of output:**

1. a **reproducible pipeline** (clean -> features -> models -> report, one command each)
2. a **leakage defence** (data contracts + 25 tests + two families of placebo checks)
3. **test results** for the three hypotheses (area-price, unit price, Metro effect)
4. a **deliverable report**, where every number traces back to a CSV

## 4. What the cleaning actually does (with measured counts)

Cleaning runs in two phases: **per chunk** and **after concatenation**.

### 4.1 Per chunk (`clean_chunk`)

| # | step | logic | why it is necessary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **transport left join** | join the four distances on a normalised postcode | must happen *before* filtering so unmatched postcodes are removed by the required-field rule rather than surviving with NA |
| 2 | **numeric coercion** | `to_numeric(errors="coerce")` on price and area | the raw file is text with blanks and junk; coercing turns "unparseable" into NaN so it can be counted instead of raising |
| 3 | **three-format date parsing** | try `%Y-%m-%d`, then `%Y%m%d`, then `format="mixed", dayfirst=True` | the file contains ISO, compact and unpadded dates; trying one format silently drops records |
| 4 | **postcode / unit-code cleanup** | strip, blank to NA | an empty string is not NaN and slips past later checks |
| 5 | **versioned development labels** | attach both windows, then derive one legality-checked `development_type` | **leakage control**: a 2005 sale cannot know a 2011-2014 vacancy share |
| 6 | **plausible date window** | keep 2001-01-01 to 2023-12-31 | the raw file contains `0015-08-29`, `1024-01-05`; these break every time-series split |
| 7 | **required fields** | price / area / area_type / contract_date / post_code / dist_cbd all present | the minimum field set; missing any of them makes the record unusable |
| 8 | **purpose filter** | `primary_purpose == RESIDENCE` | excludes commercial, industrial and agricultural transfers |
| 9 | **type filter** | `property_type == house` | excludes units, townhouses and vacant land; this project's population is detached houses |

Measured:

```
raw                       4,854,814
implausible dates           -34,130    -> 4,820,684
required-field filter    -1,478,159    -> 3,342,525
purpose filter             -793,884    -> 2,548,641
house filter               -246,371    -> 2,302,270
```

### 4.2 After concatenation

| # | step | logic | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | **whole-row de-duplication** | `drop_duplicates` | the raw file contains exact repeats (-1,114) |
| 11 | **area unit conversion** | `M` x1, `H` x10,000, unknown to NaN; `_area_unit` kept for audit | the raw `area` mixes square metres and hectares - a factor of 10,000 |
| 12 | **invalid values** | price > 0, area > 0, four distances non-negative and finite | zeros and negatives produce infinities under a log transform |
| 13 | **plausibility gate** | area 20-10,000 sqm; price $10k-$50M; unit price $10-$50k/sqm | catches **absolutely impossible** values, and must run before any statistic is fitted (removes 150,055 rows) |
| 14 | **`group_key`** | `address|postcode` | the unit of independence for CV; **never a feature** |
| 15 | **corruption fence** | fitted on the training span, applied to **every** row | removes values that cannot be a house transaction, including in years the per-year fences do not cover (removes 376,265 rows) |
| 16 | **per-year quality fence** | fitted **per year on the full sample**, applied to the **training side only** | sample-level extremes; per-year fitting is the point - see section 4.3 (flags 30,355 rows, 1.4%) |

Measured for steps 15-16:

```
entering the outlier step   2,151,094
corruption fence removes     -376,265   -> 1,774,829
per-year quality fence flags   30,355   (rows kept, training side only)
```

### 4.3 Why there are three layers, not one

This is the most important design decision in the project, and it came from two real failures.

| layer | basis | time coverage | applied to | catches |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A plausibility gate** | **domain constants** (written before looking at the data) | all years | every row | `area = 1.53e9 sqm`, `price = $875M` - **absolutely impossible** values |
| **B corruption fence** | IQR fitted on the training span (statistical) | all years (training-span bounds) | every row | values the training period already proved are not this population |
| **C quality fence** | IQR fitted **per year** (statistical) | training side only | training rows | within-sample extremes that would otherwise dominate the loss |

**Lesson 1.** Originally only layer C existed, and:
- the `area = 1.53e9` row is a 2020 contract, so **no per-year fence covered it**; it reached the
  model and made ridge predict $9.1e11 for it;
- pointing that fence at the validation set instead **silently emptied the 2016-2022 validation
  windows**, which made the metrics look *better*.

**Lesson 2 (added later).** Layer C used to be fitted on the **training span only (2001-2015)**,
so it judged 2023 sales against a **$2.34M** price ceiling while the 2023 99th percentile is
**$2.9M**. The per-year diagnostic showed **100% of every year from 2016 onward being rejected**:
the training side was empty and the "expanding window" plateaued at 1.12M rows. Layer C now fits
on the full sample per year:

| year | price ceiling before | after | share flagged |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | $2.34M (global) | $3.71M | 1.7% |
| 2019 | $2.34M (global) | $3.60M | 1.1% |
| 2021 | $2.34M (global) | $5.73M | 1.9% |
| 2023 | $2.34M (global) | $5.18M | 1.1% |

Methodologically, layer C is a **one-off dataset-level decision** (12.5% of rows trimmed, stated in
the methods) rather than a per-fold statistic refitted on the target. It never touches the target,
and it is disclosed. Using a 2001-2015 ruler on 2023 prices was the actual error.

Three layers are therefore not over-engineering: they correspond to three different failure modes.
See `03_leakage_audit.md` sections 8-10. The per-year table
(`reports/tables/iqr_bounds_by_year.csv`) would have exposed both defects on day one.

## 5. File-by-file

### 5.1 `src/utils/` - infrastructure

| file | key contents | logic and purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `config.py` | `PROJECT_ROOT`, directory constants, `CleaningPolicy`, `PlausibilityPolicy`, `SampleWindows`, `SETTINGS` | **single source of configuration.** No other file hard-codes a path or threshold. Changing a bound means editing one place - and because it is one place, it can be quoted in the report |
| `io.py` | `READ_DTYPES`, `iter_raw_chunks`, `count_rows`, `read_postcode_key`, JSON helpers | `READ_DTYPES` reads all 17 columns as strings (except postcode) because the raw "numeric" columns contain blanks and junk: **read as text, parse yourself** so the count of unparseable values is exact. `read_postcode_key` turns `"2112.0"`, whitespace and blanks into a 4-character string |

### 5.2 `src/data/` - from raw records to a clean sample

| file | key functions | logic and purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `contract.py` | `check_raw`, `check_clean`, `assert_all`, `postcode_coverage` | **the data contract**: assertions at every stage boundary, including the absolute plausibility ranges and the rule that pre-2011 contracts carry no development label. Purpose: **fail loudly when the data changes** instead of producing a wrong conclusion quietly. Note that `dev_label_legality` once mis-reported for a long time because it checked the **wrong column** - contracts need auditing too |
| `postcode_features.py` | `build_development_labels`, `attach_legal_development_type`, `build_transport_features`, `join_transport` | (1) **development labels**: vacant-land share per postcode, built **twice** (2001-2010 and 2011-2014 baselines), then reduced to one legality-checked label. (2) **transport distances**: postcode centroid to CBD / all stations / 13 Metro stations. (3) `join_transport` uses `validate="many_to_one"` plus a row-count assertion - the most careful join in the project |
| `clean.py` | `clean_chunk`, `parse_contract_dates`, `convert_area_units`, `add_group_key`, `drop_duplicate_rows`, `drop_invalid_values`, `apply_plausibility_gate`, `build_clean`, `CleaningReport` | the 16 steps of section 4. `CleaningReport` is the **audit carrier**: staged counts, missing-field profile, gate hit counts, all written to `clean.meta.json` |
| `outliers.py` | `IQRBounds`, `fit_iqr_bounds`, `fit_global_bounds`, `mark_quality`, `apply_iqr_filter` | **the three-layer design.** `fit_iqr_bounds` produces per-year fences plus a global entry; `mark_quality` emits two independent booleans: `iqr_corruption_ok` (applies to every row) and `iqr_keep` (additionally passes the per-year quality fence). Purpose: push the "who gets dropped" decision from the data layer to the modelling layer, so training and validation can use different policies |
| `macro_asof.py` | `MacroPanel`, `fetch_cash_rate`, `fetch_cpi`, `load_macro_panel`, `as_of`, `assert_macro_asof_valid`, `macro_coverage_report` | **monthly macro as-of join.** Cash rate from the RBA change log keyed by effective date (same-day effect, no publication lag); CPI from RBA G1 with an explicit 28-day publication-lag assumption, joined on `release_date <= contract_date` rather than `period_end <=`. `assert_macro_asof_valid` is a hard assertion against future information |
| `events.py` | `EventStudySpec`, `load_events`, `event_month`, `assign_treatment`, `build_event_panel`, `treatment_summary`, `_stations_at_event`, `write_spec` | **event definition and treatment assignment.** E1 opened 2019-05-26 with all 13 stations on one day, so there is a single cohort. Treatment distance prefers the **geocoded property-level distribution** (`dist_metro_p25`), falling back to the centroid for postcodes with fewer than 3 addresses, and uses only stations open at the event date. `write_spec` persists the design as JSON - that is the **pre-registration** |
| `geocode_features.py` | `load_station_entrances`, `build_postcode_geocode_distances`, `compare_treatment_definitions` | **property-level distance distribution.** From `data/geocode_results.csv` (709 postcodes, up to 10 addresses each) it computes per-postcode `dist_metro_p25/p50`, `share_within_{1,2,3,5}km`, spread and coverage. Two deliberate choices: distances are measured to **station entrances** (52 relevant entrances) rather than station centroids, and only stations **already open** at the event date are used. `compare_treatment_definitions` shows where the two definitions disagree - 6 postcodes at the 2 km threshold |

### 5.3 `src/features/` - from a clean sample to the modelling table

| file | key functions | logic and purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `panel.py` | `to_month`, `build_postcode_month_panel`, `complete_monthly_spine`, `add_rolling_features`, `attach_panel_features` | **where the lagged neighbourhood features are built** - and the file that most needs care. (1) aggregate to `postcode x month`; (2) reindex onto a complete monthly spine (gaps are explicit NaN, **never forward-filled**); (3) **`shift(1)` before rolling** - this single line is the leakage guard, ensuring month *t* only uses sales up to *t-1*; (4) require a fully observed window and emit `pc_n_months_observed_*` so coverage is explicit; (5) when joining back, **same-month statistics are forcibly renamed `contemporaneous_*`** and never reach the model |
| `pipeline.py` | `load_clean`, `clean_zoning`, `add_calendar_features`, `add_geometry_features`, `add_target`, `build_features` | **assembles the modelling frame** with deterministic transforms only: macro as-of, calendar, geometry, `log10(price)` target, panel features, zoning encoding. Anything that learns from data lives in the fold-aware transformer instead. Purpose: this table can safely be generated once over the whole history |

### 5.4 `src/models/` - models

| file | key functions | logic and purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `features.py` | `FeatureSpec`, `PropertyFeatureTransformer`, `make_xy` | **the fold-aware transformer** (sklearn style). Median imputation plus missing indicators, category frequency encoding, **smoothed target encoding** for high-cardinality categories, area quantile binning, standardisation - **all fitted on the training fold only**. `FeatureSpec` is the single place that decides what enters the model. Calendar features were once declared as low-cardinality categories here, which frequency-encoded them and made a time trend unlearnable |
| `registry.py` | `ModelSpec`, `default_model_specs`, `MODEL_ALIASES`, `select_model_specs`, `build_model`, `naive_postcode_month_predict`, `naive_global_predict` | **models and baselines.** The naive baselines are the honesty check: `naive_postcode_month` predicts the postcode's trailing 12-month median using lagged information only. It was once **double-log-transformed**, which made it look broken and flattered every model. Tree models run with `n_jobs=1` by default because joblib's thread backend needs named pipes, unavailable in some environments |

### 5.5 `src/validation/` - splitting

| file | key functions | logic and purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `time_series_cv.py` | `Fold`, `rolling_origin_splits`, `holdout_window`, `month_keys`, `split_frame`, `summarize_folds`, `assert_no_leakage` | **rolling-origin CV with group purge.** (1) 8 forward-chaining folds, optionally reserving the final N months as a holdout; (2) `split_frame` applies quality policy **asymmetrically** - the corruption gate to both sides, the per-year quality fence to the **training side only** (applying it to validation empties later windows); (3) any group key present in validation is removed from training (3.6-6.1% of rows per fold); (4) `assert_no_leakage` asserts the embargo and group isolation, and is used by the tests |

### 5.6 `src/causal/` - causal inference

| file | key functions | logic and purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `event_study.py` | `balance_panel`, `estimate_event_study`, `_demean_two_way`, `_cluster_cov`, `placebo_shifted_event`, `placebo_random_treatment`, `dose_response` | **the E1 event study.** (1) balance the panel so fixed effects absorb the same information for every postcode; (2) `_demean_two_way` absorbs postcode and calendar-month effects by alternating projections, **without materialising thousands of dummies**; (3) event-time dummies interacted with treatment identify the path, reference period omitted; (4) `_cluster_cov` gives **postcode-clustered** standard errors (strong within-postcode correlation makes row-level errors overconfident); (5) the joint pre-trend Wald test is the admission gate; (6) two placebos: shifted event date and random treatment assignment; (7) `dose_response` replaces binary treatment with continuous exposure, because only 8-11 postcodes sit within 2 km |

### 5.7 `src/evaluation/` - evaluation and tests

| file | key functions | logic and purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `metrics.py` | `rmsle_from_log10`, `dollar_metrics`, `regression_report`, `grouped_report`, `compare_models` | **metric definitions.** Headline RMSLE (log scale: sensitive to relative error, not dominated by expensive homes), plus dollar metrics via the **median** back-transform with an explicit note that this is a median, not a mean (the mean back-transform carries Jensen bias). `grouped_report` splits by year and area because an average hides "only accurate in one segment". Lesson: dollar RMSE was once **100% attributable to a single row**, so tail metrics must be reported separately. **RMSLE and MdAPE rank models in opposite order** - see the report's section 4.6 |
| `hypothesis_tests.py` | `bootstrap_correlation_ci`, `fe_regression`, `describe_bad_control`, `neighbourhood_robustness`, `test_h1_area_price`, `test_h2_unit_price` | **the H1/H2 tests.** (1) bootstrap **clustered by postcode** (row-level resampling understates uncertainty); (2) `fe_regression` supports controls and interactions with clustered errors; (3) `describe_bad_control` states why `pc_med_price_*` is a bad control (it sits mid-causal-path); (4) H2's **permutation placebo** isolates the mechanical ratio; (5) H1 is recomputed per year to show that one pooled coefficient hides a sign change |

### 5.8 `pipelines/` - the only entry points

| file | purpose | key design |
|---|---|---|
| `01_build_clean.py` | raw CSV to `clean.parquet` | builds the two postcode lookups (reusable via `--skip-reference`), cleans in chunks, fits the two fences, prints the per-year fence table, runs the contracts, writes `clean.meta.json` |
| `02_build_features.py` | clean to `features.parquet` | loads the RBA snapshots (refreshable), assembles the modelling frame, prints macro coverage by year and null counts |
| `04_train_models.py` | rolling CV + final holdout | per fold: fit the transformer, train every model, accumulate predictions; then score the holdout **once**. Key flags: `--holdout-months`, `--max-train-rows` (**0 means a true expanding window**), `--rf-trees`, `--xgb-rounds`, `--rf-jobs`, `--keep-iqr-outside` |
| `05_hypothesis_tests.py` | H1/H2 + robustness | three correlations with clustered bootstrap CIs, within-FE elasticity, ratio-bias placebo, neighbourhood-price robustness |
| `06_event_study.py` | E1 event study | treatment assignment, panel, main specification, two placebos, radius x outcome grid, dose response, figure |
| `07_make_report.py` | every figure + the English report | **the only place that draws figures**, so text and images cannot drift apart |
| `08_feature_value_test.py` | feature-value ablation | cumulative layers + permutation and time-reversal placebos + within-postcode demeaning, with XGBoost as the main model to keep ~100 fits affordable |
| `NOTES.md` | run order | explains why there is no `03` |

### Hypothesis tests

| test | result |
|---|---|
| **H1** area vs price | pooled log-log correlation -0.122; **within-postcode-and-year elasticity +0.244** (clustered by postcode) -> the pooled negative sign is a between-location artefact |
| **H1 robustness** | adding the lagged neighbourhood price moves the elasticity only +0.244 -> +0.246, so the bad-control concern is empirically second-order here; the interaction term is **-0.072 (z = -4.4)**: a hotter market flattens the area gradient |
| **H2** unit price | observed correlation -0.547, shuffled-price placebo -0.466 -> **85% is the ratio's denominator**; elasticity 0.244, far below 1 |
| **H3** Metro | pre-trend test **p < 0.001, rejected in every specification** -> no causal reading. The dose-response coefficient points the expected way (larger rises nearer a station) but is not significant at 5 km |
| **Feature value** | calendar (-0.068, 8/8 folds) > **lagged price statistics (-0.036, 7/8)** > lagged unit price (-0.002, 5/8) ~ liquidity (-0.001, 4/8) |

### Models (8 pooled folds / final holdout)

| model | pooled RMSLE | holdout RMSLE | holdout MdAPE |
|---|---|---|---|
| random_forest | **0.3321** | **0.3560** | 17.7% |
| naive (postcode trailing 12-month median) | 0.3650 | 0.3785 | **16.1%** |
| xgboost | 0.3478 | 0.3946 | 22.9% |
| linear_ridge | 0.3764 | 0.4347 | 27.2% |

**The most important sentence.** Random forest wins 7 of 8 folds, but on the **untouched 2023
holdout** the gap narrows to 0.022 and the naive benchmark's median error is **better**. A
53-dimensional feature set buys a consistent but modest improvement over one number - the local
recent median. Any claim that it adds a lot would be overstated.
